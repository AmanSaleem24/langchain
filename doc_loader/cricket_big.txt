When dawn breaks softly o’er the ground,
And sleepy streets awake to sound,
A wooden bat, a leather ball,
Can make a nation rise or fall.

The grass is green, the sky is wide,
Eleven hearts stand side by side,
The pitch lies silent, smooth and bare,
Yet dreams of millions gather there.

A bowler walks with measured pace,
Determination on his face,
He grips the ball, he takes his run,
A battle starts, a contest won?

The arm swings high, the ball takes flight,
A flash of red against the light,
The batter waits, his eyes like flame,
One fleeting second shapes the game.

A crack! The ball goes soaring high,
It cuts a pathway through the sky,
The crowd erupts, the boundaries ring,
And every heart begins to sing.

Four runs race along the ground,
Six runs send the ball unbound,
The fielder leaps, the rope draws near,
A million voices shake the air.

Yet cricket is not merely runs,
Nor just the clash of bats and guns—
Well, not of guns, but nerves of steel,
And all the pressure players feel.

For every glorious cover drive,
A hundred quiet hopes survive,
For every wicket, every cheer,
There lies a tale of sweat and fear.
I. The Art of Batting
O batter, master of the crease,
Who seeks both glory and release,
Your willow speaks a language rare,
Of timing, courage, skill, and flair.

The forward stride, the steady head,
The judgment of the ball ahead,
A straight drive clean as morning rain,
A pull shot roaring through the plain.

The cut that slices, sharp and quick,
The sweep that makes the spinners sick,
The lofted shot, a fearless flight,
That sends the ball beyond our sight.

Yet batting asks for more than might,
More than a swing, more than delight;
It asks the mind to read the seam,
To turn a moment to a dream.

A maiden over, dot by dot,
Can test a player more than thought;
The scoreboard stalls, the tension grows,
While patience fights the fear that flows.

A hundred runs, a century made,
A monument that will not fade,
Each single earned, each boundary struck,
Each risk survived, each twist of luck.

And when the bat comes down at last,
The crowd remembers every blast;
For centuries are not merely scores—
They are the opening of doors.
II. The Bowler's Song
O bowler, artist of the seam,
Who bends the laws of batter's dream,
You charge the pitch with fire and grace,
And dare the strongest to give chase.

The fast bowler, thunder in his stride,
With speed and anger held inside,
A yorker aimed at stumps below,
A bouncer rising like a blow.

The outswinger that leaves the bat,
The inswinger that bends in flat,
The slower ball, the hidden change,
That makes a settled batter strange.

The spinner waits with patient eyes,
And whispers secrets through the skies;
A leg-break turning from the right,
A doosra veiled from plainest sight.

The flight, the drift, the dip, the turn,
The subtle lessons batters learn;
A fraction's width, a moment's doubt,
And off-stump cartwheels inside out.

A wicket falls, the bowler roars,
The fielding side awakens scores;
Ten fingers rise, the crowd takes flame,
And one more soul departs the game.

But bowling holds a crueler truth:
Some days defy the strongest youth;
Ten overs bowled, no wicket won,
Yet still the next ball must be done.

For greatness isn't only taking,
Nor only records worth the making;
It is the courage to return,
To face the loss, to live and learn.
III. The Fielders and the Keeper
Across the field, the guardians stand,
With open eyes and steady hands;
Each blade of grass, each patch of ground,
Becomes a kingdom to defend and guard.

The slip cordon, alert and still,
Awaits the edge with sharpened will;
One tiny nick, one fleeting sound,
And suddenly the game's turned round.

The cover fielder reads the play,
The midwicket guards the other way;
At point, a diving figure flies,
And dust rises beneath the skies.

A catch is taken, clean and high,
Ten thousand voices fill the sky;
A single hand, a single leap,
Can wake a nation from its sleep.

The keeper crouches, quick and keen,
The sharpest eyes you've ever seen;
A stumping flashes, bails take flight,
A blink too slow, a wrong-foot night.

And when the ball escapes the hand,
A fumble shakes the waiting stand;
But fielders know, as all must do,
That every error offers something new.

For cricket gives no perfect day,
No flawless path, no certain way;
It asks us all, when chances fall,
To rise again and give our all.
IV. The Symphony of Formats
Test Cricket — The Longest Trial
Five days beneath the changing sun,
Where patience battles till it's done,
The whites grow stained, the faces tired,
Yet still the deepest hopes burn fired.

A session won, a session lost,
Each wicket bears a hidden cost;
The pitch wears cracks, the shadows creep,
While captains calculate and keep.

The red ball moves, the seam grows worn,
And fresh-born strategies are born;
A defensive block, a leave outside,
Can show a strength no six can hide.

A match drawn out through rain and time,
May hold a beauty most sublime;
For sometimes, though no winner stands,
Respect is earned by both the hands.

Then comes the final morning's play,
When victory may be one step away;
A last-wicket stand, a target chased,
A nation's hope in one breath placed.

Test cricket teaches how to wait,
How discipline can challenge fate;
How character, through long days tried,
Can stand when every dream has died.
One-Day Cricket — The Balanced Art
Fifty overs, a measured race,
Where strategy must match the pace;
Build the innings, guard the score,
Then lift the tempo, ask for more.

The new ball swings, the middle slows,
The pressure builds as daylight goes;
The final overs, the roaring crowd,
The batters stand both brave and proud.

A hundred in a World Cup game,
Can carve a lasting place in fame;
A spell of five, a stunning catch,
Can turn the course of any match.

The chase begins, the target glows,
Each calculated single flows;
The asking rate climbs like a wall,
And every dot can change it all.

The final over, six runs due,
The bowler knows what he must do;
One full toss flies, one boundary lands,
And joy or grief fills waiting stands.
T20 Cricket — The Thunderbolt
Twenty overs, a blazing sky,
Where every ball can make dreams fly;
The batter swings, the crowd explodes,
And every over shifts the roads.

The powerplay, a fearless start,
Where boldness is a practiced art;
The middle overs, spinners spin,
While clever batters search to win.

The death overs arrive in haste,
No precious delivery goes to waste;
A ramp, a scoop, a reverse sweep,
And fielders wonder where to leap.

The leagues bring stars from distant lands,
United by the game and fans;
New partnerships, new skills unfold,
As cricket writes its tales in gold.

Yet in this rush of lights and sound,
Where sixes rain upon the ground,
The oldest truth remains the same:
A single ball can change the game.
V. The Captain's Burden
A captain stands beyond the crease,
Seeking a path through war and peace;
He sets the field, he reads the skies,
And weighs the truth behind disguise.

Should slips remain or boundaries guard?
Should tired legs attack as hard?
When should the spinner take the ball?
When must the strongest batter fall?

He watches clouds, he studies pitch,
He knows the game can turn in a switch;
A bowling change, a field moved wide,
May bring a wicket, shift the tide.

But leadership is more than plans,
More than applause from cheering fans;
It is the hand upon a shoulder,
When pressure makes a teammate older.

It is the trust that does not break,
The choice to learn from each mistake;
The courage to accept the blame,
And share the glory of the game.

For captains, like the rest of us,
Must lead through doubt, through noise, through fuss;
Their greatest triumph may be found
In lifting others from the ground.
VI. The Twelfth Player — The Crowd
Beyond the rope, a sea of faces,
A thousand hopes in countless places;
Some wear their colours, some wave flags,
Some clutch old shirts and weathered bags.

A child sits wide-eyed by the stand,
A painted flag held in his hand;
He watches heroes walk the green,
And dreams of days he's never seen.

An old man speaks of matches past,
Of innings built to forever last;
He tells the young of legends' names,
Of famous wins and crueler games.

The crowd can lift a tired soul,
Can make a distant target whole;
A thunderous roar, a rising chant,
Can give a team the strength it lacks.

Yet silence, too, can pierce the air,
When wickets fall and none can bear
To watch the scoreboard change its face,
As victory slips beyond their place.

For cricket binds the near and far,
The village lane, the shining star;
From dusty fields to stadium light,
It brings a thousand hearts upright.

And though the fans may disagree,
On players, calls, or strategy,
They share a love no score can measure,
A living, breathing, priceless treasure.
VII. India and the Cricketing Heart
Across the fields of Bharat's land,
The game is held in every hand;
On narrow streets and dusty squares,
A child begins his dream upstairs.

A broken bat, a tennis ball,
A brick for stumps against a wall;
A friend who bowls, a friend who bats,
A game that needs no grand contracts.

The afternoon grows hot and long,
Yet every shot becomes a song;
A neighbour shouts, a window shakes,
The ball disappears through garden gates.

From Mumbai's streets to Delhi's ground,
From Chennai's roar to Kolkata's sound,
From Bengaluru's rain-washed green
To Ahmedabad's enormous scene—

The nation gathers, eyes alight,
When India walks into the fight;
The blue shirts shine, the anthem plays,
And hope grows taller through the haze.

The tricolour flutters in the breeze,
The crowd erupts beneath the trees;
A billion dreams, both young and old,
Are wrapped in blue, in hope, in gold.

When Tendulkar's straight drive flew,
When Dravid stood steadfast and true,
When Dhoni finished under lights,
When Kohli chased through endless nights—

When Rohit sent the white ball high,
When Bumrah's yorkers split the sky,
When new young heroes found their flame,
Each era added to the game.

And when the World Cup dream is near,
The nation's heartbeat becomes clear;
Every run and every fall
Feels larger than the field itself.

But cricket's soul is not one name,
Nor one immortal, shining frame;
It lives in every player who
Dares to begin and follow through.

It lives in women who have fought
For recognition long unsought;
Who struck their boundaries, claimed their place,
And changed the future of the race.

For cricket's field belongs to all,
To those who rise and those who fall;
To every dreamer, every child,
Whose love of play remains undefiled.
VIII. The Agony of Defeat
Not every match will end in cheers,
Not every dream outlives its fears;
Sometimes the finest plans collapse,
And victory slips through narrow gaps.

A mistimed shot, a missed-out run,
A catch dropped underneath the sun;
A no-ball called, a review denied,
And hope retreats, with grief beside.

The batter walks with lowered head,
The dressing room grows still as lead;
The scoreboard tells a brutal tale,
Of chances missed and courage frail.

A final ball, a narrow loss,
The dreams of months reduced to dross;
The crowd grows quiet, faces pale,
As disappointment tells its tale.

Yet defeat is not the end of play,
Nor does it take your worth away;
It shows the cracks that must be healed,
The lessons hidden in the field.

The greatest champions have known
The bitter taste of losing alone;
They've faced the press, endured the blame,
Then risen to reclaim their name.

So let the tears fall where they may,
Let grief have room to have its say;
For those who dare to risk their all
Will sometimes stumble, sometimes fall.

And when the next match calls again,
Beyond the loss, beyond the pain,
The bat will rise, the bowler run,
And hope will greet the morning sun.
IX. The Spirit of Cricket
Beyond the records, runs, and fame,
There lies the heart beneath the game;
A code of honour, fair and true,
That asks the best of me and you.

To walk when edged, though none may see,
To play with grace and dignity;
To shake the hand, accept the call,
And give respect to one and all.

For winning gained through hollow means
Can never match the noblest dreams;
A trophy may command the eye,
But honest conduct will not die.

The umpire stands, impartial, still,
To judge each moment with fair will;
Two raised fingers, one raised arm,
Can fill a heart with joy or harm.

And when tempers rise and words grow sharp,
When competition strikes its spark,
Remember that the game's embrace
Is greater than a moment's race.

For rivals may compete all day,
Yet share respect along the way;
The finest contest, fierce and fair,
Leaves admiration everywhere.

The spirit lives when players know
That how they play matters as much as how they go;
That victory shines a brighter light
When earned with honour, skill, and right.
X. The Final Over
Now evening falls upon the ground,
The floodlights glow, the fans surround
The pitch where dreams have dared to fly,
Beneath a darkening velvet sky.

The target stands, the runs are few,
The bowler knows what he must do;
The batter grips his willow tight,
And time seems frozen in the night.

First ball: a single, calm and neat.
Second: a yorker, hard to meet.
Third ball: a boundary, struck away.
The crowd erupts, then waits to pray.

Two runs to win, three balls remain,
A hush descends like summer rain;
The bowler turns, the batter stares,
And tension fills the waiting air.

He runs in hard, the ball is sent,
A mighty swing, a moment bent;
The ball climbs high, the fielders chase,
While time slows down across the place.

A catch? A six? A twist of fate?
A breathless crowd can hardly wait.
The ball comes down beyond the rope—
And suddenly the world is hope.

The players run, their arms spread wide,
Their years of toil now justified;
The captain lifts the silver prize,
As tears of joy fill countless eyes.

Or perhaps the catch is taken clean,
And silence sweeps across the scene;
The victors leap, the others mourn,
And dawn prepares for dreams reborn.

For that is cricket: joy and pain,
The sun, the dust, the wind, the rain;
A thousand endings, one refrain—
Tomorrow, we shall play again.
XI. The Legacy
Long after players leave the ground,
Long after cheers have faded down,
The game remains, alive and bright,
A fire that glows through every night.

A father teaches his child to bowl,
A mother cheers with all her soul;
A teacher sets a makeshift crease,
And gives a restless heart its peace.

The village team, the college side,
The local heroes filled with pride;
The first selection, the first defeat,
The first time dreams and hard work meet.

Some will rise to international fame,
Some will never leave the local game;
But every player who has tried
Has found a world they hold inside.

For cricket teaches patience, too,
And what a little faith can do;
It teaches teamwork, grit, and grace,
And how to start from any place.

It teaches us that talent grows
Where discipline and effort flow;
That luck may smile, then turn away,
But preparation earns its day.

It teaches us to stand our ground,
To hear the truth in silence found;
To trust our team, to take our turn,
To fail, reflect, improve, and learn.

And when the years have changed our stride,
When youth and speed have left our side,
We'll still recall that open field,
The dreams that time could never yield.

The smell of grass, the evening air,
The bat in hand, the friends who care;
The shouted runs, the stumps of wood,
The simple joy of playing good.

For cricket is more than sport or score,
More than the trophies that we adore;
It's childhood's echo, friendship's flame,
A living story with no final name.
XII. The Unending Innings
So let the bowler mark his run,
And let the batter face the sun;
Let fielders spread across the green,
And let the next great play begin.

Let young hands learn the proper grip,
Let hopeful hearts refuse to slip;
Let every village, town, and street
Hear leather, willow, rhythm meet.

Let women rise and children play,
Let barriers fall along the way;
Let every voice be heard and seen,
Let every field remain a dream.

Let records break and legends fade,
Let fresh new heroes be displayed;
For no one owns the game alone—
Its truest home is every home.

And when the final stumps are drawn,
When one more match has passed and gone,
When players leave and crowds depart,
The game remains within the heart.

For cricket is the art of flight,
Of courage in the fading light;
Of hope that lives through every fall,
Of finding strength when chances are small.

It is the patience of the Test,
The one-day chase that gives its best,
The T20's explosive flame,
The quiet love behind the game.

It is the bowler's fierce intent,
The batter's hours of patient bent,
The fielder's leap, the captain's call,
The crowd that rises over all.

It is the lesson, old and true,
That dreams belong to me and you;
That though the world may change its frame,
Some hearts will always love this game.

So raise the bat and praise the ball,
Raise your voice and give your all;
Let every generation claim
Its rightful place within the game.

And when the sun sinks out of sight,
And stars take over from the light,
One truth will echo, clear and plain:

The game will live. The dreams remain.

For as long as children dare to play,
As long as dawn brings a new day,
As long as hearts can bear the flame,
The world will rise for cricket's name.

And somewhere, on a dusty street,

A child hears willow, leather meet.

He dreams of crowds, of flags unfurled—

And cricket carries on,

Across the world.
